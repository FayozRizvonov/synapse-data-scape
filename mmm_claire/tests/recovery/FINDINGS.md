# What the recovery test found

Run 2026-11-04. Both modes **FAIL**. Data generated by the model's own equations, at the
demo's sample size (T=33 monthly), with no misspecification whatsoever.

```
                              raw     mean
parameters informed by data   7/26    7/26     (27%)
coverage, informed only       1.00    1.00     (7/7)
coverage, all parameters      0.77    0.81     <- inflated, see below
max R-hat                     1.0000  1.0000
divergences                   0       0
in-sample r2                  0.738   0.776
achievable r2 given noise     0.670   0.712
```

## 1. The headline: 19 of 26 parameters never left their prior

Identical in both scaling modes. Posterior sd within 10% of prior sd — and for six of
them *wider* than the prior — for:

- all 6 `beta_media` (media response)
- all 6 `theta_media` (carryover / decay)
- all 6 `gamma_media` (saturation)
- `intercept`

The θ and γ posteriors match their priors to three decimal places:

| | prior mean | prior sd | prior 90% CI | posterior, both modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| θ | 0.406 | 0.127 | [0.136, 0.500] | mean 0.393–0.414, sd ~0.127, CI ~[0.12, 0.50] |
| γ | 1.041 | 0.490 | [0.300, 2.000] | mean 1.034–1.111, sd ~0.475, CI ~[0.31, 2.00] |

True θ spanned 0.05–0.40 and true γ spanned 0.4–1.5. Every channel came back at the
prior mean regardless. **Adstock and saturation are not being estimated. They are being
reported back from the prior**, which means the stored adstock and saturation curves —
and the decay rates the dashboard presents as findings — carry no information from the
data.

## 2. Sampling is not the problem

Max R-hat 1.0000, zero divergences, min ESS 1324. The sampler explored the posterior
correctly. It found the right answer to the question it was asked; the question carries
no information about these parameters. No amount of extra draws, chains or
`target_accept` will change this, and the stability controller would not have escalated
— its diagnostics all pass.

## 3. Media scaling is real but **not** the root cause

`pymc_mmm.py:112` feeds raw `X_media` into `X/(γ+X)` with γ clipped to ≤2.0, while
`build_tensors` computes `X_media_scaled` that **no model, curve, contribution, ROI or
effectiveness module ever reads**. On the real dovato data that flattens every channel:

| channel | raw CV | x_sat mean | signal surviving |
|---|---|---|---|
| mass_email | 0.183 | 0.999945 | 0.01% |
| clm_call | 0.439 | 0.999314 | 0.24% |
| non_clm_call | 0.446 | 0.999005 | 0.33% |
| email_1to1 | 0.188 | 0.998852 | 0.12% |
| webinar | 0.398 | 0.975922 | 1.67% |
| phone_call | 1.107 | 0.976174 | 1.87% |

That is a genuine defect. But normalising media (`--media-scale mean`) restores 24–53%
of the signal and **every media parameter is still prior-only**. Scaling is necessary and
nowhere near sufficient. An earlier reading of this evidence treated scaling as the root
cause; the contraction measurement does not support that.

## 4. The actual root cause: 31 free parameters, 33 observations

```
intercept, beta_trend, seasonality_sin, seasonality_cos,
base_beta, delta_mid, delta_upper,
beta_media_raw[6], theta_media_raw[6],
gamma_global, tau_gamma, gamma_offset[6],
beta_control[3], sigma                        = 31 free parameters
```

Against 33 monthly observations (G=SB=1). Three parameters per channel — response,
carryover, saturation — is simply not identifiable from 33 points, and the funnel
hierarchy plus the `0.6·log(spend_share)` prior shift then supply the answer the data
cannot. The design matrix is not at fault: worst pairwise correlation among the
saturated regressors was 0.54.

The low-spend channels show the prior doing the work most clearly. Even in `mean` mode:

| channel | spend share | prior shift | true β | posterior β | bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| email_1to1 | 0.019 | −2.37 | 0.456 | 0.131 | −4.5 sd |
| mass_email | 0.027 | −2.17 | 0.570 | 0.158 | −4.7 sd |

Confidently wrong: narrow intervals, far from truth, driven by the spend-share shift.

## 5. What this means for the stored outputs

Media contributions, channel ROI, VRR, adstock curves and saturation curves are
**prior artifacts**. They encode the declared funnel ordering and the spend split, so
they are close to a restatement of `spend.csv` presented as a model result. This is
consistent with what the demo has been showing — implausible symposium figures, empty
base/ROI cells, channel numbers that would not reconcile.

In-sample r² **exceeded the achievable ceiling** in both modes (0.738 vs 0.670; 0.776 vs
0.712). The model overfits base, trend, seasonality and controls while media contributes
nothing. `fit_metrics.r2` is therefore not merely uninformative here — it is actively
misleading, and it rises as media gets worse.

## 6. What does work

Worth stating plainly, because it is the part that can be trusted:

| parameter | learned | bias | recovered |
|---|---|---|---|
| `beta_trend` | 89% | −0.7 sd | yes |
| `beta_control[comp1]` | 94% | −0.5 sd | yes |
| `beta_control[ex_1]` | 41% | −0.1 sd | yes |
| `beta_control[ex_2]` | 45% | +0.7 sd | yes |
| `seasonality_sin` / `cos` | 52% / 54% | ≤0.3 sd | yes |
| `sigma` | 75% | −0.7 sd | yes |

7/7 coverage. **Event dummies work correctly** — `ex_1` (annually recurring) and `ex_2`
(a sustained four-month block) both recover, so declaring an event as `type=control` in
`info.csv` does what it should. That answers the dummy-variable question directly: the
capability exists and is sound; it is only the *UI* for choosing variables that is
decorative.

The `intercept` is the one base parameter that fails (prior-only, +4.4 sd off in raw
mode). Its true value is −4.21 because saturated media injects a constant of ~4.2 that
the intercept must offset, and `intercept ~ Normal(0, 1)` cannot reach it. Under
normalisation the conflict halves (+1.7 sd). A second, smaller instance of the same
unscaled-input problem.

## Caveats on this test

- **Media is weak relative to base in this design.** True media share of signal variance
  was 0.0002 (raw) and 0.0291 (mean), so β recovery was being asked a hard question
  partly of the generator's making. The θ/γ results are unaffected — those are
  scale-free and their posteriors equal their priors exactly — but a follow-up should
  raise media's share to make β recovery a fair test on its own.
- **Coverage over all parameters is meaningless here** and is reported only to show the
  inflation: γ scored 6/6 "ok" purely because its 90% interval spans the entire
  admissible range [0.3, 2.0] and therefore cannot miss. This is why the verdict binds
  on informed-parameter coverage plus the share of parameters informed.
- One seed, one sample size. `--seed` and `--periods` are exposed; a sweep over periods
  would locate the sample size at which media becomes identifiable.

## Suggested order of work

1. **Normalise media** by its own mean before the model (not the mean-centred
   `X_media_scaled`, which goes negative and would be clamped by the `1e-6` clip).
   Necessary, insufficient, and it moves every stored number including the demo's r².
2. **Reduce media parameters per channel.** Three per channel on 33 points is the
   binding constraint. Pooling θ and γ across channels, or fixing them, converts 18
   parameters into ~2–8.
3. **Fix the θ prior/cap collision.** `Beta(2,2)` has mean 0.5 and `theta_cap` is 0.5, so
   50% of prior mass is censored onto the ceiling — the cap and the prior's centre are
   the same number.
4. **Re-run this test after each** and require the informed-parameter count to rise.
   `run_recovery.py` exits non-zero on FAIL, so it can gate changes to `pymc_mmm.py`.

None of the above has been applied — this test only measures. Every change in that list
alters stored model outputs and therefore the client-facing demo, so they are decisions
to take deliberately rather than consequences of a diagnostic.
